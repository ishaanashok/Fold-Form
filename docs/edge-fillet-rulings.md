# Edge fillet rulings

1. A tap targets a welded mesh vertex within 14 screen points, then a sharp edge within 11 points. An edge is sharp when its adjacent face normals differ by roughly 20 degrees; a ray check rejects hidden back edges. This avoids selecting cap triangulation seams. **Cost if wrong:** very small or gently curved edges may need a different tolerance or an explicit selection mode.
2. A supported fillet rebuilds one convex corner of a single-loop prism. An edge rounds that extrusion corner for its full length; a vertex rounds the corner near its cap and tapers it back to the original profile. The generated mesh remains closed. **Cost if wrong:** irregular bodies, holes and previously deformed bodies can be selected visually but do not start a fillet; a general solid fillet kernel would be needed for them.
3. The incoming hinge angle uses `HingeInputManager`'s existing clamp and snap unchanged. Radius grows linearly from zero to half the body's smallest dimension at a 180-degree bend, and is capped by local adjacent edge lengths. **Cost if wrong:** the radius can feel small on a thin plate; a different size scale would alter the feel without changing angle sensitivity.
4. A tap selects an edge or vertex until the next viewport tap or long press. Tapping the highlighted feature again, or tapping elsewhere, deselects it; at a non-flat angle this commits one document feature and freezes its shape until the hinge returns to flat. Hold commits the same feature explicitly. **Cost if wrong:** switching directly to a different feature takes a second tap, and someone expecting deselection to discard a live fillet will instead keep it.
5. The existing `undoEditButton` is Revert for the left toolbar; it reverses exactly one edit. **Cost if wrong:** if Revert was meant to reset the entire design, that destructive action remains in the dropdown as Reset everything.
6. Share stays mounted while its format menu is open. Choosing a format closes the dropdown; the share sheet is anchored to the editor so it survives that collapse. **Cost if wrong:** someone who expects the dropdown to close as soon as Share opens will see it behind the format menu until they choose a format or tap away.
7. A press on the viewport, view cube, or a primary tool dismisses the dropdown without adding a full-screen hit-testing layer. **Cost if wrong:** tapping a noninteractive status overlay may leave the dropdown open until the next viewport press.
8. The edge and vertex screen tolerance applies just outside a body silhouette too. **Cost if wrong:** two bodies with nearly overlapping outlines can be ambiguous when the tap ray misses both.
